# 2025: The Only Constant Is Change

As the title suggests, 2025 was a year of enormous change for me. Through all its ups and downs, I gradually came to understand a simple idea: “Wherever I go, that is where I am.” This sense of ease did not appear from nowhere. I learned it through change after change—by settling myself into the present and allowing my body and this very moment to become my true point of grounding.

Fear no longer dictates my steps. I do my best with what I can change, and I learn to let go of what lies beyond my control instead of exhausting myself by turning it over again and again. I bring my attention back to what is in front of me, stay with each unfolding moment, and try to make every day feel fully alive: remaining curious about the world while appreciating everything I already have. Change itself, as the saying goes, is the only constant.

## 1. Work

Yes, I changed jobs yet again. The issue was not so much the job itself as my desire for a different environment and way of life. During my time in Shanghai, both the pace and my social circle gradually began to feel dull. Before long, I found myself thinking that it was time to make a move. And so I came to Hangzhou.

My new company works on software-defined radio. FPGA development here mainly involves RF data acquisition and front-end signal-processing algorithms. Overall, Hangzhou—especially Binjiang—does move at a noticeably faster pace, and there is considerably more overtime. Even when the workday is supposed to be over, the office towers remain brightly lit 😂. The pay is fairly good, though, so for now I plan to keep my feet on the ground and do the work well, continuing to explore my career path within this new rhythm.

## 3. Meditation

I still did not achieve “enlightenment” this year, but I did establish the habit of meditating every morning. In some ways, meditation is much like physical training: its real effects come from long-term, consistent practice, not from a single flash of insight.

Once it becomes a habit, subtle changes in one's state of mind begin to reveal themselves in everyday life. The most obvious change for me is a clearer awareness of my emotions. When anger arises, I notice much sooner that “I am getting angry.” And when I place my attention on observing it, the emotion is less able to sweep me away. It is still there, but it no longer controls me completely.

The inner changes brought by meditation are often slow and delicate, sometimes so subtle that they are difficult to notice. Yet these small moments of release are like a lever: over time, they can move much larger parts of life.

## 4. Snowboarding

This was my second snow season. I have taught myself almost entirely—the cost of lessons is simply too high, often reaching 500 yuan for a single hour. I have always felt that a teacher is not necessarily required to get started with many things; self-study can work perfectly well. Online resources are now so abundant that anyone willing to spend time selecting good material and practicing can obtain excellent guidance.

I learned most of my snowboarding through Bilibili. Many skilled riders have produced systematic beginner courses that start from zero, with each lesson focusing on one movement or technique. The best creators break every exercise into very small steps, so you know exactly what to practice on the next run, how to practice it, and what level you should reach before moving on. I love the feeling of practicing something again and again and then seeing the improvement with my own eyes. The feedback from learning is immediate. Perhaps that is one reason snow sports are sometimes called “white opium”: you are continually pulled forward by those moments when you realize, “I just got a little better again.”

From stumbling around an indoor snow center at the beginning to now being able to link turns with reasonable fluency on the intermediate slopes at Songhua Lake, I have come to love the moments when I glide through vast, snow-covered mountains. In my ears are the soft scraping sound of the board's edge cutting into the snow and the rush of the wind as I pick up speed. In that moment, almost nothing remains but concentration: attention to my body, rhythm, center of gravity, and the surface beneath me. Immersed in the mountains, my mind instead becomes very quiet, and I can clearly feel a kind of peace that I have not experienced for a long time.
